📖 Read the chapter

Angles measure turn. A few facts unlock most problems: on a straight line they add to 180°, around a point to 360°, and in a triangle to 180°.

An angle measures how far something has turned. Two straight arms meet at a point called the vertex, and the angle is the amount of turn from one arm to the other, measured in degrees (°). A full turn all the way round is 360°, so a quarter turn — a right angle — is 90°.

The five kinds of angle

The size of an angle is not the length of its arms

  • An angle's size depends only on how much it has turned, never on how long the arms are drawn. For example, a 30° angle is 30° whether its arms are 1 cm or 10 cm long — only the gap between the arms counts.
  • Because of this, a small angle with long arms can look bigger than a large angle with short arms. Always read the turn, not the ink.

Four facts that solve almost every angle problem

  • On a straight line, angles add to 180°. For example, if one angle on a straight line is 120°, the other is 180 − 120 = 60°.
  • Around a point, angles add to 360°. For example, if three angles meeting at a point are 150°, 90° and an unknown, the unknown is 360 − 150 − 90 = 120°.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal. When two straight lines cross they make an X shape, and the two angles facing each other across the X are always equal. For example, if one angle at the crossing is 70°, the angle directly opposite it is also 70° (and the other two are each 110°).
  • The angles inside a triangle add to 180°, and inside any quadrilateral they add to 360°. For example, a triangle with angles 90° and 60° has a third angle of 180 − 90 − 60 = 30°.

Two extra names describe pairs that add to a fixed total: angles are complementary if they add to 90°, and supplementary if they add to 180°. For example, 35° and 55° are complementary; 35° and 145° are supplementary. (Alphabetical order helps: c before s, 90 before 180.)

Angles in parallel lines

Parallel lines never meet, and are marked with matching arrowheads. A line that cuts across them is called a transversal, and it creates pairs of angles that are linked no matter how far apart the parallel lines are.

  • If one angle is 70°, its corresponding angle and its alternate angle are both 70°, while its co-interior partner is 180 − 70 = 110°.
  • When one transversal crosses two parallel lines it makes eight angles, and every one of them is either the angle you were given or 180° minus it — nothing else. If your answer is neither, check the pairing again.

How to find a missing angle

  1. Name the pattern. Is the angle on a straight line, around a point, across an X, inside a triangle, or between parallel lines?
  2. Write the total that pattern gives you: 180°, 360°, or "equal to".
  3. Subtract the angles you already know from that total.
  4. Check it looks right. Compare your answer with the picture — an angle that looks acute should come out under 90°.

🦺 A skate ramp

A ramp meets flat ground at 25°. Since angles on a straight line add to 180°, the angle on the other side is 180° − 25° = 155°.

🏠 A roof's pitch

A symmetric roof's two slopes meet at 110° at the peak. The three angles of that triangle add to 180°, so the two equal base angles share 180° − 110° = 70° between them: each is 35°.

🧭 A ship's bearing

A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from north, always written as 3 digits (e.g. 060°). A ship sails on a bearing of 60°, then turns to a bearing of 150° — a turn of 150° − 60° = 90°, a quarter-turn clockwise.

Why 180° in a triangle? Tear off the corners and lay them along a straight edge — they always form a straight line. Parallel-line angles are equal because the slopes never change.
⚠ Watch outComplementary angles add to 90°; supplementary add to 180°. Mixing these two up is the most common angle error.
⚡ Mental maths strategyAlmost everything is 180 or 360: straight line 180°, triangle 180°, around a point 360°, quadrilateral 360°. Subtract what you already know.

🕵️ Mistake detective

Diagnose the misconception, then reveal the cause and intervention.

On a straight line, one angle is 110°, so the other is 90°.
Cause

They assumed a right angle instead of using the straight-line fact.

Diagnostic question

What rule or representation would show why this answer cannot be right?

Intervention

Angles on a line add to 180°, so the other is 180 − 110 = 70°.
