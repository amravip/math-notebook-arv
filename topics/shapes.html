Shapes are classified by sides and angles. The angle-sum rules let you find missing angles in any polygon.

A polygon is a closed flat shape made only of straight sides — and it is named by how many sides it has: 3 triangle, 4 quadrilateral, 5 pentagon, 6 hexagon, 8 octagon. A polygon is regular when all its sides and all its angles are equal.

Triangles are named twice over

  • By their sides: equilateral (all three equal), isosceles (exactly two equal), scalene (none equal).
  • By their biggest angle: right-angled (one 90° angle), acute (all three under 90°), obtuse (one over 90°). Both names can apply at once — a triangle can be right-angled and isosceles.
  • Equal sides force equal angles. In an isosceles triangle the two angles opposite the equal sides are equal, and every angle of an equilateral triangle is 180 ÷ 3 = 60°. For example, an isosceles triangle with a 40° apex has base angles of (180 − 40) ÷ 2 = 70° each.

The quadrilateral family

Most quadrilaterals form a nested family: trapezium → parallelogram → rhombus and rectangle → square. Each one is a special case of the one before it, and a special case keeps every property of its parent. That is why a square is still a rectangle, still a rhombus and still a parallelogram — but a rectangle is not a square. (The kite is the exception: it sits outside that chain entirely.)

Angle sums: why (n − 2) × 180°

  • Interior angles of any polygon add to (n − 2) × 180°. For example, a hexagon (n = 6) totals (6 − 2) × 180° = 720°.
  • In a regular polygon every angle is equal, so divide that total by n. For example, a regular hexagon: 720° ÷ 6 = 120° per angle.
  • Exterior angles — the turn you make at each corner walking round the outside — always total 360°, for every polygon. So one exterior angle of a regular polygon is 360° ÷ n.
  • At each corner the interior and exterior angles sit on a straight line, so interior + exterior = 180°. For example, a regular octagon: exterior = 360° ÷ 8 = 45°, so interior = 180° − 45° = 135° — which matches (8 − 2) × 180° ÷ 8. Two routes, same answer.

One angle of a regular polygon

  1. Count the sides, n.
  2. Exterior angle = 360° ÷ n — usually the quicker route.
  3. Interior angle = 180° − exterior angle.
  4. Check: a regular quadrilateral (a square) gives exactly 90°, and the angle grows towards 180° as n gets bigger — it can never reach it.

🛑 A stop sign

A stop sign is a regular octagon (8 sides). Interior angles total (8−2)×180° = 1080°, so each angle is 1080° ÷ 8 = 135°.

🐝 A honeycomb

Bees build honeycomb from regular hexagons because a hexagon's 120° angles fit three-to-a-point (3 × 120° = 360°) and tile a flat surface with no gaps — only triangles, squares and hexagons can do this alone.

🏗️ A roof truss

A triangular roof truss stays rigid, while a plain quadrilateral frame can collapse sideways — three fixed side lengths lock a triangle's angles, which is why builders brace structures with them.

Why (n − 2) × 180°? Any polygon splits into triangles from one corner: an n-sided shape makes (n − 2) triangles, each carrying 180°.

Cause

They multiplied by n, but the rule uses (n − 2).

Diagnostic question

What rule or representation would show why this answer cannot be right?

Intervention

(5 − 2) × 180° = 3 × 180° = 540°.
