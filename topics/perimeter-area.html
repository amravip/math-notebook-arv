✦How much fencing for a yard? How much paint for a wall? Perimeter measures the edge, area measures the inside — two very different questions people mix up all the time.

Before any rules: just look. What do you notice? What do you wonder?

Two paddocks each use exactly 40 m of fence: one is 10×10, the other 18×2.

You might notice…

  • Both use identical amounts of fence
  • The space inside is wildly different: 100 m² vs 36 m²

You might wonder…

  • Which shape gives the most space for a fixed fence?
  • Can a shape’s perimeter grow while its area shrinks?

Hold on to those wonderings — this chapter answers most of them.

A rectangle is 5 cm by 3 cm. Count the squares inside, then walk around the edge. Which count needs multiplying and which needs adding?

Inside (area) = 5 × 3 = 15 squares. Around (perimeter) = 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 cm. Area multiplies; perimeter adds.

perimeter
the total distance around a shape
area
the space inside, in square units
compound shape
a shape made of simpler shapes joined together

Perimeter is the distance around a shape (add the sides). Area is the space inside (in squares). Compound shapes split into rectangles and triangles.

Perimeter is the distance around the edge of a shape — a length. Area is the space inside it — a covering, counted in unit squares. They measure different things, so they are never interchangeable.

Two questions about one shape

  • Perimeter and area are independent. A 1 × 8 rectangle has perimeter 18 but area 8, while a 4 × 4 square has a smaller perimeter (16) and a bigger area (16).
  • Area units are always squared: cm², m², km². An answer to an area question without the square is only half an answer.

Where the formulas come from

  • Rectangle = length × width. Triangle = ½ × base × height. Parallelogram = base × height. For example, a triangle with base 8 cm and height 5 cm has area ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm².
  • A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides (a and b, h apart) and area ½(a + b) × h — average the parallel sides, then multiply by the height. For a = 6, b = 10, h = 4: ½ × 16 × 4 = 32.
  • "Height" always means the perpendicular height, measured at a right angle to the base. Using the slanted side is the most common wrong answer in this chapter.
  1. Decide which question is being asked — edge or inside, cm or cm².
  2. For a compound shape, split it into rectangles and triangles, and mark every length you know on the diagram.
  3. Find the missing lengths from the ones given — opposite sides of the whole shape must still agree.
  4. Work out each piece, then add (or subtract, for a cut-out), and write the units.

🚧 Fencing a garden

A rectangular garden is 8 m by 5 m. Fencing needs the perimeter: 2 × (8+5) = 26 m of fence.

🧹 Carpeting a room

A room is 4 m by 3.5 m. Carpet needed is the area: 4 × 3.5 = 14 m² — carpet is bought by area, not by the length of the walls.

📌 Two paddocks, same fence

A 10 m × 10 m paddock has perimeter 40 m and area 100 m². An 18 m × 2 m paddock also has perimeter 40 m, but only 36 m² — the same fencing can enclose very different areas.

Why length × width? You're counting unit squares that tile the shape. A triangle is half its surrounding rectangle, hence ½ × base × height.
⚠ Watch outPerimeter and area are different measures. A shape with a bigger perimeter can have a smaller area.
⚡ Mental maths strategyRectangle perimeter in your head: add length and width once, then double. 8 by 5 → 13, doubled → 26.

Diagnose the misconception, then reveal the cause and intervention.

Area of a 5 × 3 rectangle = 16 cm².
Cause

That's the perimeter (adding the sides), not the area.

Diagnostic question

What rule or representation would show why this answer cannot be right?

Intervention

Area = length × width = 5 × 3 = 15 cm².
