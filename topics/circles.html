Every circle links its radius to two measures: circumference C = πd (distance around) and area A = πr² (space inside). π ≈ 3.14 is the same ratio for every circle.

A circle is every point that sits the same distance from one centre point. That distance is the radius, and it is the number nearly every circle question is really about — so if a question hands you anything else, turn it into the radius first.

The parts of a circle

  • Diameter and radius convert in one step: d = 2r and r = d ÷ 2. For example, a 30 cm plate has a radius of 15 cm.
  • π (pi) is what you get when you divide any circle's circumference by its diameter — about 3.14. Every circle in the universe gives the same answer, which is exactly why one formula fits them all.
  • Because C ÷ d = π, multiplying back gives C = πd, and since d = 2r that is the same as C = 2πr. The two forms are one formula, not two.

The two formulas — and how to pick

  1. Check what you were given — radius or diameter? If it is the diameter, halve it before doing anything else.
  2. Pick the formula from around-versus-inside.
  3. Substitute and calculate, using π ≈ 3.14 (or the calculator's π key).
  4. Write the units, squared for area. Then sanity-check with π ≈ 3.
  • For a circle of radius 5 cm: C = 2 × 3.14 × 5 = 31.4 cm, and A = 3.14 × 5² = 3.14 × 25 = 78.5 cm². Note that r² means square the radius first, then multiply by π.
  • The most common slip is putting the diameter into A = πr². A 10 cm-wide circle has area π × 5² = 78.5 cm², not π × 10² = 314 cm² — four times too big.
  • The perimeter of a half-circle is not just half the circumference: you have to add the straight edge back on. For a semicircle of radius 5 cm, perimeter = ½ × 31.4 + 10 = 25.7 cm.

🚴 A bike wheel

A bike wheel has a 66 cm diameter. Every full turn rolls the bike forward one circumference: π × 66 ≈ 207 cm, just over 2 metres per turn.

🍕 Comparing pizza deals

A small pizza (20 cm across) costs $10; a large (30 cm across) costs $18. Area scales with r², so the large has (15÷10)² = 2.25× the area for only 1.8× the price — better value.

🏃 A running track's lanes

A 400 m track's lanes are curved. Runners in outer lanes start further forward, because a bigger radius means a bigger circumference (C = 2πr) to cover the same number of laps fairly.

🮁 Why A = πr²?

Slice a circle into thin wedges and lay them out alternating point-up, point-down. The zigzag strip is roughly a rectangle: height r, width half the circumference (πr) — area = πr × r = πr².

Why π everywhere? π is the fixed ratio of any circle's circumference to its diameter. Because it's constant, one number scales every circle.
⚠ Watch outArea uses r² and circumference uses r once — and check whether you’re given the radius or the diameter before substituting.
