2Learn

Volume is the space inside a 3-D solid (cubic units). Surface area is the total area of all faces. For a cuboid, volume = length × width × height.

Volume measures how much space a 3-D solid fills, in cubic units. Surface area measures the total area of its faces — the filling versus the skin.

Volume is layers of unit cubes

  • For any prism, volume = base area × height. For example, a box with base 3 × 2 = 6 and height 4 has volume 6 × 4 = 24 cm³.
  • Capacity is the same measurement wearing a different unit: a cube 10 cm on each side holds 1000 cm³, which is defined as 1 litre.
  • To find a side length back from a volume you undo the cube. For 64 cm³: 3³ = 27 is too small, 4³ = 64 fits — so the side is 4 cm.
  • Scaling behaves differently in 3-D: double every edge of a cube and the volume becomes 8 times bigger (2³), not twice. A 2 cm cube (8 cm³) becomes a 4 cm cube (64 cm³).

Surface area: three pairs, add, then double

  1. Decide which is wanted: how much it holds is volume; how much wraps it is surface area.
  2. For volume, find the base area, then multiply by the height. Units are cubed.
  3. For a cuboid's surface area, note the 6 faces come in 3 matching pairs. Find one from each pair, add the three, then double. For a 4 × 3 × 2 box: 12 + 8 + 6 = 26, doubled = 52 cm².
  4. Check the units — cm³ for volume, cm² for surface area. Getting these the wrong way round is the fastest way to spot your own mistake.

🐟 Filling a fish tank

A tank is 60 × 30 × 40 cm: volume = 72,000 cm³. Since 1000 cm³ = 1 litre, that's 72 litres of water to fill it.

📦 A shipping box

A box is 50 × 40 × 30 cm (60,000 cm³). Doubling every dimension to 100 × 80 × 60 cm doesn't just double the volume — it makes it 8 times bigger (480,000 cm³).

🎁 Wrapping a gift

Wrapping paper needed is the box's surface area (every face added up); how much it can hold is its volume — two different questions about the very same box.

📦 Unfolding a cereal box (4 × 3 × 2 cm)

Top & bottom: 4×3 = 12 cm² each. Front & back: 4×2 = 8 cm² each. Left & right: 3×2 = 6 cm² each.

SA = 2×(12 + 8 + 6) = 2×26 = 52 cm² — the flattened net above shows exactly these 6 pieces.

Why cubic units? Volume counts unit cubes filling the solid — base area × layers high. Surface area sums the flat faces, the material to wrap it.
⚠ Watch outVolume is cubic units, surface area is square units. Doubling a cube’s side multiplies its volume by 8 (2³), not 2.
⚡ Mental maths strategyCount one layer, then stack: a 3 × 4 base is 12 cubes, so 5 layers make 60. Base × height, always.

Diagnose the misconception, then reveal the cause and intervention.

A cuboid’s volume is written as 24 cm².
Cause

They used square units (cm², for area) where volume needs cubic units (cm³).

Diagnostic question

What rule or representation would show why this answer cannot be right?

Intervention

Volume fills space in three directions, so it is measured in cubic units: 24 cm³. Square units (cm²) are for surface area — the flat covering.
