Metric units scale by powers of 10 (1 km = 1000 m). Compound measures like speed combine two quantities (distance ÷ time), and one triangle relationship finds any of the three.

The metric system runs on powers of ten, so converting between units never involves anything harder than moving a decimal point. The skill is knowing which way it moves.

The ladder of units

  • 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm; and for capacity, 1 L = 1000 ml. Converting to a smaller unit always gives a bigger number.
  • The same ladder works for mass (kg, g, mg) and capacity (L, ml), because the prefixes mean the same thing everywhere.
  • Mixing units mid-calculation is the classic slip. Convert everything to one unit first, before any arithmetic.

Rates and the speed triangle

  • A rate combines two different kinds of unit — km per hour, dollars per kilogram, mg per kg of body weight.
  • speed = distance ÷ time, rearranging to distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. For example, 150 km in 3 h is 50 km/h.
  • Units must match before you calculate. For 24 km in 30 min, convert 30 min = 0.5 h first: 24 ÷ 0.5 = 48 km/h. Dividing by 30 gives a nonsense answer.
  • Speed has two common outfits. To convert km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6 (1000 m ÷ 3600 s), so 90 km/h = 25 m/s.
  1. Write down what unit the answer must be in before you start.
  2. Convert every quantity into matching units — minutes to hours, cm to m.
  3. Pick the arrangement from the triangle and substitute.
  4. Sanity-check the size: a car at 48 km/h is believable, one at 0.8 km/h is not.

🍳 Following a recipe

A recipe needs 250 ml of milk, but your jug is marked in litres. Since 1 L = 1000 ml, 250 ml = 0.25 L — converting first avoids pouring the wrong amount.

✈️ Planning a road trip

A trip is 360 km at 90 km/h. Time = distance ÷ speed = 360 ÷ 90 = 4 hours — but only once both are already in matching units.

💊 Reading a medicine dose

A label says 5 mg per kg of body weight. For a 20 kg child, that's 5 × 20 = 100 mg — mixing up mg and g here could mean a dose 1000 times too strong.

🚴 A cyclist's mixed-unit speed

A cyclist rides 24 km in 30 minutes. 30 minutes is 0.5 hours — convert the time first: speed = 24 ÷ 0.5 = 48 km/h. Dividing 24 by "30" (forgetting the unit switch) gives a wrong, much smaller answer.

Why speed = distance ÷ time? It's 'distance per one unit of time'. Rearranging gives distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed.
⚠ Watch outConverting to a smaller unit gives a bigger number. And always match units (hours with hours) before you calculate.
⚡ Mental maths strategyConverting to a smaller unit? Expect a bigger number. Metres to centimetres must give more centimetres (×100), never fewer.

Cause

They multiplied by 10 instead of 100 (there are 100 cm in a metre).

Diagnostic question

What rule or representation would show why this answer cannot be right?

Intervention

2.5 × 100 = 250 cm.
